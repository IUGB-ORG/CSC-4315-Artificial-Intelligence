Part 1 · 10 minutes

Why a dense network on pixels does not scale

64
inputs — session 16 digits
150,528
inputs — one 224 × 224 colour image
150 million
weights in one dense layer of 1,000 neurons
28
numbers in one 3 × 3 colour filter

A 224 × 224 colour image is 224 × 224 × 3 = 150,528 numbers. Connect that to a hidden layer of 1,000 neurons and the first weight matrix alone holds over 150 million numbers — before the network has learned anything, and with no idea that neighbouring pixels belong together or that a cat in the corner is still a cat.

What a convolutional filter does about it

Slide one small set of weights — 3 × 3 × 3 plus a bias, 28 numbers — across the whole image, computing the same weighted sum at every position. Weight sharing makes it cheap; locality makes it look at neighbourhoods; and a pattern detected in one place is detected everywhere. Stack such layers and early ones find edges, later ones textures, later still parts of objects.

Part 2 · 20 minutes

Transfer learning: borrow the eyes, train the verdict

Training a good image network from scratch takes a very large labelled dataset and a lot of compute. A clinic in Grand-Bassam with 400 photographs has neither. But the early and middle layers of a network trained on ImageNet — 1.2 million photographs in 1,000 categories — have learned things that are useful for almost any photograph: edges, textures, shapes, parts.

Keep what was learned about seeing; replace only the final decision
1
Load a pretrained network — Lab 16 uses MobileNetV3-Small, built to be light enough for phones.
2
Freeze its feature extractor: requires_grad = False for every parameter, so backprop leaves them alone.
3
Replace the last layer — 1,000 ImageNet outputs — with a new one that has two.
4
Train only what is not frozen, for a few epochs, on your small dataset.
The same move you already know

This is session 16's training loop with most parameters switched off. The backbone turns each image into a feature vector; the new head is a small classifier on those features — close to the neuron from session 11. You are training a small model on very good features someone else learned.

📐
Use the backbone's own preprocessing

The pretrained network expects images resized, cropped and normalised exactly as during its original training. weights.transforms() supplies that pipeline. Feeding it differently scaled images is session 19's scaling bug, in a new costume.

The code, and what to change

The notebook is provided and works. Your job is the data.

the heart of Lab 16python
model = models.mobilenet_v3_small(weights=models.MobileNet_V3_Small_Weights.DEFAULT)
for p in model.features.parameters():
    p.requires_grad = False                                       # frozen
model.classifier[-1] = nn.Linear(model.classifier[-1].in_features, 2)   # new head

opt = torch.optim.Adam([p for p in model.parameters() if p.requires_grad], lr=1e-3)

Only the classifier layers after the feature extractor are trainable — a few hundred thousand parameters out of about 1.5 million, most of them in the classifier's hidden layer. Three epochs on 400 CIFAR-10 images of cats and dogs typically moves validation accuracy from chance to well above it. Then change CLASSES, or point the notebook at your own folder of photographs.

Local data

Forty photographs per class is enough to see transfer learning work: two local crops, two kinds of market produce, two types of road damage. The session-5 grid you sliced by hand is the same object as every one of these images — just larger, and in colour.

Part 4 · 10 minutes

When transfer learning works, and when it does not

Works well when…Struggles when…
the new images resemble photographsthe images are very different: X-rays, satellite bands, microscopy
classes differ in visible shape or texturethe difference is subtle and needs expert features
you have tens to hundreds of images per classyou have a handful, or wildly unbalanced classes

When the images are very different, unfreeze the last block or two and train them with a small learning rate — the lab's extension — or look for a backbone pretrained on similar images. And every lesson of unit 4 still applies: a test set touched once, a baseline, and the question of which mistakes cost more.
